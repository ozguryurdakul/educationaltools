    All results

    What the tool does

    • Confined concrete after Mander, Priestley & Park (1988): confinement effectiveness ke, effective lateral pressures f′lx, f′ly, confined strength fcc from the five-parameter William–Warnke surface (equal and unequal confinement), stress–strain curve (Eq. 3) and ultimate strain εcu,c.
    • Moment–curvature of the fibre section (200 strips + bars) under a constant axial load N, top fibre in compression.
    • N–M interaction by three methods, both bending directions.

    Working with the tool

    • Enter the inputs and press COMPUTE: material curves, moment–curvature and N–M are calculated together. Until then the status bar and the button show that the results belong to the previous inputs.
    • The cross-section drawing follows the inputs immediately, so the bar layout and ties can be checked before computing.
    • Save Project writes all inputs (bar tables and ties included) to a .json file; Open Project reads it back, then press COMPUTE.
    • Export Excel / JSON and Save Figures always give the results of the last COMPUTE.

    Units and signs

    • Input in MPa and mm; section results in kN, kNm and 1/m.
    • Axial load N is positive in compression. Positive moment puts the top fibre in compression.

    Section and confinement

    • The confined core is measured to the hoop centreline (Mander 1988, Appendix II): bo = b − 2·cover − øhoop, ho = h − 2·cover − øhoop, ds = D − 2·cover − øhoop. Untick the box to enter your own values.
    • ke (Eq. 22) uses the clear spacings w′ between laterally restrained bars only: corners, bars of a tied middle line, and top/bottom bars on a vertical tie. Untied bars are drawn grey and the arch spans past them.
    • A vertical tie must hook a bar at both ends, so it is possible only at an inner top-line bar that has an inner bottom-line bar at the same x (e.g. 5 bars on top and 3 at the bottom: only the middle tie). The other rows of the vertical-tie table are locked (“no bottom bar”).
    • Ties have the hoop diameter. Bar lines are evenly spaced from top to bottom.

    Materials

    • Unconfined (cover) concrete: Mander curve up to εcu; if εcu > 2εco it descends linearly to zero between 2εco and εcu, otherwise it drops to zero at εcu (spalling).
    • Confined ultimate strain: Priestley et al. (1996) εcu,c = 0.004 + 1.4ρsfyhεsuh/fcc, Mander's energy balance (Usfρs = ∫fcdε + ρcc∫fsldε − 0.017√fco; 110 MJ/m³ is Mander's value for mild-steel hoops), or a user value.
    • Reinforcement: elastic–perfectly plastic, bilinear hardening, or Mander (1984) with a yield plateau to εsh.

    Moment–curvature

    • The extreme compression fibre strain is increased step by step; the neutral-axis depth follows from axial equilibrium and φ = εtop/c.
    • Confined core + cover: confined Mander curve in the core, unconfined curve in the cover (it spalls at εcu); ultimate when the extreme core fibre reaches εcu,c. Unconfined section (also used automatically when ke = 0): unconfined curve everywhere; ultimate when the extreme section fibre reaches εcu. Both also stop when the extreme tension bar reaches εsu. Concrete in tension is ignored.
    • Key points (Priestley, Calvi & Kowalsky 2007): first yield (tension bar at εy or concrete at 0.002), nominal Mn (concrete at 0.004 or tension bar at 0.015), φy = φ′y·Mn/M′y, μφ = φu/φy.

    N–M interaction

    • From M–φ (Mander): Mmax, Mn and M′y of the moment–curvature analysis for about 23 axial loads.
    • Strain compatibility: Mander laws of the M–φ option; extreme fibre at εc,lim or extreme tension bar at εsu; a fully compressed section turns about the strain of the peak axial load.
    • EC2 design: parabola-rectangle concrete (fcd = αccfck/γc, with fck = fco), bilinear steel fyd = fy/γs, pivots B and C of EN 1992-1-1. The minimum eccentricity (6.1(4)) is not applied.

    Limitations

    • Uniaxial bending about one axis, monotonic loading; no bar buckling, shear or cyclic behaviour.
